A GOOD PLACE TO BEGIN

Your study-abroad plan, made simple

You do not have to decide everything today. Start by writing down what you know, what matters most to you and what you still need to find out.

01

Start with your goal

Think about the subject you enjoy, the qualification you want and the kind of work you may want to do later.

02

Compare courses and destinations

Look beyond the country name. Compare course content, entry requirements, duration, costs and student support.

03

Make a realistic budget

List tuition, living costs, travel, insurance and other likely expenses. Check current amounts from official sources.

04

Check the requirements early

Ask the institution about documents and deadlines. Verify visa requirements using the destination government's official guidance.

QUICK ANSWERS

More guides, in plain English.

APPLICATIONS · 4 MIN

Application documents: a simple checklist

Prepare a working folder for your passport details, academic records, English-language evidence if required, CV or personal statement if requested, references if needed, and any forms the institution specifies. Requirements vary, so always use the official checklist for your course.

SCHOLARSHIPS · 5 MIN

How to start looking for scholarships

Search university funding pages and reputable scholarship providers. Check eligibility, award value, covered expenses, application requirements and closing dates. Never assume an award is guaranteed, and be cautious of anyone promising funding in exchange for an upfront fee.

STUDY ABROAD · 5 MIN

Choosing a destination beyond the photos

Compare the course itself, total cost, accommodation, transport, climate, support services and distance from your support network. Ask how the qualification fits your longer-term plans, and confirm course details directly with the institution.

APPLICATIONS · 4 MIN

Visa preparation: start with official guidance

Visa requirements can change and depend on your destination and circumstances. Use the relevant government immigration website, check the latest document list and financial evidence rules, and keep copies of submitted documents. BCAS guidance should support—not replace—official requirements.

STUDENT LIFE · 5 MIN

Building a realistic student budget

Estimate housing, food, transport, phone, study materials, insurance and personal spending. Include initial costs such as deposits and travel, and leave room for unexpected expenses. Use current local information rather than relying on a single estimate.
